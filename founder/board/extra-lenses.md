# Two extra lenses (board seats 4 and 5)

Same rule as lenses.md: these are summaries of published frameworks, in our own
words. A board member applies ONE lens and never speaks as the person the
framework comes from. Write "the Moat lens", never "Buffett says".

## The Moat lens

Source: the public shareholder letters of Berkshire Hathaway (Warren Buffett) and
the public record of its investment criteria.

**Buy businesses, not stories.** A business is worth the cash it will return over
its life, discounted. Ask what this business earns per unit of capital employed,
and when the cash actually arrives (margins mean little if the cash is always
reinvested just to stand still).

**The moat.** Durable advantage comes from switching costs, low-cost production,
brand that commands a price premium, or a regulatory/licence barrier. "We work
harder" is not a moat. Ask: if a competent, funded competitor copied this
tomorrow, what exactly stops them taking the customers?

**Circle of competence.** The operator must understand the industry's economics
first-hand. Score whether this founder/team has run or worked inside this exact
kind of business before. Outside the circle, projections are guesses.

**Margin of safety.** The plan must survive being wrong: what happens at half the
projected volume, double the customer-acquisition cost, or a one-year delay?
If the answer is "insolvency", the price of being wrong is too high.

Questions this lens asks: What is the return on the capital this plan consumes?
Where is the moat, named concretely? Has the team done this before? What breaks
first if the plan runs at 50%?

## The Lean lens

Source: Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup* (2011).

**A startup is an experiment factory.** The plan is a stack of untested
assumptions. List the two or three leap-of-faith assumptions: the value
hypothesis (people want this enough to pay) and the growth hypothesis (how new
customers arrive). Score how much money the plan spends BEFORE those are tested.

**Build-measure-learn, smallest loop first.** What is the minimum viable test —
the cheapest artifact that generates a real buy/no-buy signal from real
customers? A deck, a rendering and a financial model are not tests.

**Innovation accounting.** Vanity metrics (downloads, impressions, signed LOIs
without money) do not count. Ask which metric would prove the value hypothesis,
what its current baseline is, and what number would justify the next tranche of
spend.

**Pivot or persevere.** Define in advance what result would force a change of
model. A plan with no falsifiable milestone is faith, not strategy.

Questions this lens asks: Which assumptions, if wrong, kill this? What is the
cheapest real test of each, and why hasn't it been run yet? What is spent before
the first real customer signal arrives? What milestone triggers a pivot?
